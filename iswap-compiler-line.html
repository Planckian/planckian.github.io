Gate Compilation · XY Hardware

Eq. (7) · iSWAP → SWAP by Global √X Interleaving

The Compiler's Chain The hardware gives you iSWAP, not SWAP. Transport needs a true exchange. Build each SWAP out of three iSWAPs and three global √X pulses — one bond at a time, all the way down the line.

What you are doing

An open chain of qubits with XY nearest-neighbour couplings. Bonds alternate between two global colours: α on the even bonds, β on the odd bonds.

The problem Firing a colour applies iSWAP — not SWAP. iSWAP exchanges the two states but leaves a relative i on the singly-excited sector, so it does not transport a qubit cleanly. You must synthesise the exchange yourself, and the identity you are building is the one printed in the paper — Eq. (7) of the main text, Eq. (S21) of the Supplemental Material: SWAP = (I⊗√X) iSWAP (√X⊗I) iSWAP (I⊗√X) iSWAP         ⑥       ⑤      ④       ③      ②       ① It is a matrix product, so the rightmost factor acts first: you fire ① then ② … then ⑥. That is the only translation between the paper and your fingers.
  1. Four pulses, that is all. iSWAP on α, iSWAP on β, global √X on the even qubits, global √X on the odd ones. The √X drives address a whole sublattice — you never touch one qubit.
  2. Work one bond at a time. The active bond is boxed. Fire six pulses in the right order and the accumulated 4×4 operator on that bond becomes SWAP; the qubit hops one site.
  3. Watch the matrix, not the clock. The live 4×4 and its overlap with SWAP tell you exactly where you are. Nothing is scored on faith — the block completes when the operator is the exchange.
  4. Do not interleave colours. Firing the other colour mid-block couples one of your two qubits to a third one outside the bond. The block operator stops factorising, and you have to rewind.
  5. Walk it to the end. Then the last panel prints the entire correct sequence you just built, pulse by pulse.

This is Eq. (7), not a lookalike

The block you fired is the paper's identity verbatim — Eq. (7) of the main text, Eq. (S21) of the Supplemental Material. Written as a matrix product and fired as a pulse train, side by side:

as printed SWAP = (I⊗√X) iSWAP (√X⊗I) iSWAP (I⊗√X) iSWAP ⑥ ⑤ ④ ③ ② ① as fired ① iSWAP ② √Xᴮ ③ iSWAP ④ √Xᴬ ⑤ iSWAP ⑥ √Xᴮ

Nothing is reordered or re-derived: a product acts right to left, so factor ① is the first pulse. In the paper's own words each tensor factor acts globally either on the even-parity qubits or on the odd-parity ones — which is exactly why the two √X buttons address sublattices rather than qubits. The identity is originally Schuch and Siewert's natural-XY-gate construction, refs. [37, 40] there.

Every completed block above is labelled with which reading of Eq. (7) you actually fired.

Why six, and why these six

The XY interaction run for Jτ = 3π/2 gives iSWAP, which is SWAP followed by a diagonal phase: iSWAP = SWAP·diag(1,i,i,1). That phase is harmless for a two-qubit gate you were going to track anyway, but fatal for transport — you would carry a growing phase on the excited sector at every hop.

√X does not commute with that diagonal, so sandwiching iSWAPs between global √X pulses lets the unwanted phases interfere away. Three iSWAPs is the minimum: an exhaustive search over all words in {iSWAP, √X⊗I, I⊗√X} finds nothing shorter than six letters, and exactly four words of length six work — in time order (leftmost fired first):

iSWAP · √Xᴬ · iSWAP · √Xᴮ · iSWAP · √Xᴬ iSWAP · √Xᴮ · iSWAP · √Xᴬ · iSWAP · √Xᴮ √Xᴬ · iSWAP · √Xᴮ · iSWAP · √Xᴬ · iSWAP √Xᴮ · iSWAP · √Xᴬ · iSWAP · √Xᴮ · iSWAP

All four land on SWAP up to the global phase e^{−iπ/4}, which no measurement can see, and all four are Eq. (7) in disguise. The second line is Eq. (7). The first is its A↔B mirror, legitimate because SWAP is symmetric under exchanging the two qubits. The third and fourth are the same two words fired in reverse: every factor here — iSWAP, √X⊗I, I⊗√X — is a symmetric matrix, so reversing the product transposes it, and SWAPᵀ = SWAP. That reversed reading is the one that starts with a √X on A, then iSWAP, then √X on B. The pairing is forced either way: the √X must alternate sublattices and never repeat on the same side twice running.

What it costs you

With a coupler that also mediates ZZ the whole thing collapses to SWAP = (S†⊗S†) iSWAP CZ — Eq. (8) of the main text, Eq. (S19) in the SM. Two entangling pulses instead of three, because CZ supplies the missing diagonal directly instead of grinding it out through interference.
